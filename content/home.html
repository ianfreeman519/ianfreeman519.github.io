Computational Plasma Physics

When ordinary matter is heated to high temperatures, far beyond the boiling point, atoms become ionized and form a plasma. 99% of the universe is in the plasma state: stars, interstellar gas, accretion disks around black holes, and nearly everything in between. Because of its charged nature, plasmas react to electromagnetic fields, which makes them behave very differently from ordinary gases. This is a challenging state of matter to model, and requires many approximations and simplifications to study. My work focuses on modeling plasmas in the fluid limit (highly collisional, usually high density relative to system size) and I work with simulations of magnetized plasmas in the magnetohydrodynamical (MHD) limit. My previous work has focused on modeling strongly coupled plasmas (extremely high density, like in neutron stars) capable of forming crystal structures.
